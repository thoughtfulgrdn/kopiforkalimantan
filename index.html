why should i care about Kalimantan?

While it’s easy to think of Kalimantan as somewhere far away, it’s one of our closest neighbours, and what happens there doesn’t stay there.

Beneath many of Kalimantan’s forests lie peatlands: thick layers of waterlogged plant matter built up over thousands of years. These peatlands are among the most effective carbon stores on the planet. However, this also means that when they dry out and catch fire, they can smoulder underground for weeks, releasing vast amounts of carbon into the atmosphere. In bad fire seasons, these fires become a significant contributor to global emissions. In other words, the fires in Kalimantan should not be viewed as simply another regional disaster; rather, they’re part of the climate crisis that affects us all.

As with most crises, the burden isn’t shared equally, and Kalimantan is no different.

The people who have been hit first and hardest are none other than the local communities who have contributed the least to the problem. Families breathe toxic smoke for weeks on end, children miss school, and the elderly and the sick struggle to breathe. Communities also lose the forests their livelihoods have long depended on. Moreover, many of the people fighting these fires are local volunteers, working long hours in dangerous conditions with limited equipment, because the fires are burning on their doorstep and someone has to step up.

Beyond the catastrophic human impact, the wildlife suffers too. Kalimantan’s forests are home to orangutans and countless other species found almost nowhere else on Earth, and every fire shrinks the space they have left.

That’s why we believe climate action is a shared responsibility. Those of us with more resources and more distance from the flames are in a position to help, and with that comes a duty to act. Supporting the people on the front lines, protecting the forests that keep carbon in the ground and safeguarding the wildlife that calls them home are all part of the same fight.

This home cafe is our small way of doing that. Every cup and every bake helps fund firefighters on the ground through Borneo Nature Foundation, as well as the protection of wild orangutans and their forests through Save Wild Orangutans. While it won’t solve everything, it’s a start, and together, small acts add up.
